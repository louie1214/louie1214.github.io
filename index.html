About

I'm a senior network engineer carrying lead responsibilities, with 10+ years designing, implementing, testing, and supporting large-scale IP/MPLS, Internet, Metro Ethernet, and enterprise network services. My work sits at the seam between design and operations: taking a proposed capability from a high-level design, through lab regression and field trial, into a validated production rollout that an operations team can actually run.

A lot of that comes down to repeatability. I build golden configuration deployment and standardized change automation so the same change lands the same way every time — less manual effort, fewer variances, cleaner compliance posture across the commercial network.

The other half is translation. I write the technical standards, MOPs, test plans, and operational handoffs that let design engineering, service assurance, and field teams work off the same page, and I'm the escalation point when something doesn't behave the way the design said it would.

I came up through the support floor — Tier 2 enterprise and wholesale carrier support, first-responder alarm monitoring, access engineering — which is why I tend to design for the person who has to troubleshoot it at 3 a.m.

What I'm good at

  • Turning a vendor capability into a validated, documented, deployable standard
  • Lab regression testing, FOA and field trial design
  • Automating golden configs and standardized changes with Ansible and Python
  • Risk assessment on rollout plans — and the workarounds when risk is real
  • Methods of procedure teams can execute without a conference bridge
  • Being the SME other engineers escalate to

Ansible Flex

A local web UI for running and managing Ansible playbooks — the operator-facing front end I wanted for network automation work. Pick a playbook and inventory from a managed Git-backed source, run it with live streaming output, and keep a filterable history of every run with task-level results and one-click re-run.

It reaches real infrastructure the way engineers actually have to: SSH SOCKS5 tunnels through a jump host with MFA support, credentials held in the OS keychain rather than a database, and an in-browser SSH terminal that can target a device by hostname or straight out of an Ansible inventory. Everything runs on localhost; nothing leaves the machine.

What it does

  • Playbook runner — live streaming output, task-level results, re-run from history
  • Sources — local and Git-backed playbook/inventory repos with sync
  • Credentials — SSH keys, passwords, and tokens in the OS keychain, never the DB
  • SOCKS tunnel — SSH jump-host proxying with MFA, reusable as named profiles
  • Containerlab — build and deploy Nokia SR-OS topologies, then generate inventory from the running nodes
  • FlexParse — structured extraction via TTP, TextFSM, or JMESPath, with template generation
  • Environments — managed Python venvs with pip packages and Galaxy collections
